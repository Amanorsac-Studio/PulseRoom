# Amanorsac voice clone - reference recording script

Four parts, about a minute each. Read it the way you would say it to someone
standing next to you at the desk. Not announcer voice, not reading voice.

Record each part as its own file. Each one uploads as a separate sample on the
Amanorsac profile, with that part's text as its `reference_text`.

Numbers are written the way they are spoken - "thirty to forty hertz", not
"30-40 Hz" - because the transcript has to match the audio. Read it as printed
and the two agree.

If you fluff a line, just start that part again. A minute is cheap.

---

## PART 1

Most of the problems people bring me are not mixing problems. The arrangement
is fighting itself, and no amount of EQ is going to settle that argument. So
before I touch a single fader I listen all the way through once, and I write
down the three things that bother me most. Usually it is the low end. Usually
it is the kick and the eight oh eight sitting in the same forty hertz, and
neither of them winning. You can hear that on a laptop speaker, even though
neither of them is really there. The fix is almost never a boost. It is
deciding which one owns the bottom, and then getting the other one out of the
way, properly, not politely.

## PART 2

When I do start moving things, I start with the gate on the kick. Set it to
reduce by ten to fifteen decibels, not to silence, so the tail ends before the
next transient lands. Then a high-pass at thirty to forty hertz, because rumble
you cannot hear is still eating headroom. The compressor after that should be
doing very little. Four to one, maybe two or three decibels, twenty
milliseconds of attack so the click survives. If it stops sounding like a kick,
I have gone too far, and I take it back. That happens more often than I would
like to admit.

## PART 3

Vocals are where people lose their nerve. They compress once, hear it working,
and then compress again because more felt better. Two compressors each doing
three decibels will sound smoother than one doing six, and that is not a trick,
it is just how the release times stack up. Slow one first, three to one. Fast
one second, four to one. Then ride the fader underneath both of them, by hand,
line by line. And if the chorus still does not lift, the problem was never the
vocal. It was everything standing in front of it.

## PART 4

Does it translate? That is really the only question that matters at the end.
Check it on a phone speaker, check it in the car, check it on the worst
headphones you own. If the bass disappears on the phone, saturate it so the
harmonics carry the note, because the fundamental was never going to be
reproduced at all. Add a little at two hundred to six hundred hertz and keep it
dry. And if you have been at this for six hours, stop. Your ears are lying to
you by then. The mix you fix tonight is the one you undo tomorrow morning.

## PART 5

Read this one as a warning. Slower, lower, like you are stopping someone from
making a mistake.

Careful here. Gate it too hard and the kick loses its body completely. Cut too
much at four hundred and the whole thing goes hollow on you. Watch the low mids
when you add that second guitar, because two of them at three hundred hertz is
mud, and you will not hear it until the vocal comes in. If the chorus feels
smaller after you compressed the bus, bypass it and listen again. Louder is not
bigger. It never was.
